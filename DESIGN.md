# Design direction — 晋见山乡

## Product character

A field archive for Shanxi village memories: part cultural atlas, part listening room, part co-creation notebook. The visual system combines editorial storytelling, mineral-pigment illustration and northern Chinese material textures.

## Information architecture

Eleven independent views with persistent desktop navigation: overview, real map, story archive, image atlas, timeline, voices, routes, 3D spatial model, contribution studio, saved stories, fieldwork guide. On small screens the navigation becomes a compact bottom bar. A story detail is its own view rather than a modal or an anchor farther down one long landing page.

## Visual system

- Rice-paper ground, ink-black text, clay-red accents, weathered olive and loess ochre.
- Large Chinese serif headings and precise compact metadata.
- Shanxi visual vocabulary: gray-brick courtyard walls, loess cave dwellings, stone lanes, dryland crops, village opera stages and hand-worked objects.
- Generated story artwork appears in grouped story cards and full-size details; the project keeps original local vector illustrations as lightweight fallbacks.
- Clear labels distinguish verified geographic basemap, city-level sample markers, unverified village-level proposals and scene-based sample writing.

## Signature interactions

City selection recenters a real OpenStreetMap embed. Story archive filters without leaving the page; story details include long-form sample narratives, metadata, related entries, text-to-speech and local bookmarks. Timeline filtering, audio queue and speed controls, field-route selection, local contribution drafts and a code-built WebGL model form separate task surfaces.

## Accessibility and motion

Responsive navigation, semantic landmarks, visible keyboard focus, labeled controls, readable contrast and reduced-motion handling. The 3D model has equivalent clickable node controls in a side list.

## Content truth

The 11 city entries are provincial navigation, not proof of village fieldwork. City-centre map pins are approximate browse locations. Story texts, time labels and routes are experience prototypes until interviews, permissions, coordinates and local review are complete. The 3D model is conceptual rather than surveyed.
